# ADHD Notes & Insights

Personal notes on ADHD, inspired by Mark Manson's video on his experience with ADHD and medication.

## Personal Context

- Meds work for me similarly to how Manson describes - they help with focus and getting things organised
- I don't think I've lost my personality or creativity on meds (unlike Manson's experience)
- But I definitely notice when I forget to take them - "all over the shop" is a good way to put it!
- It's worth being aware of the trade-offs even if my experience differs from his

## Key Insights from Mark Manson's Video

### The Trade-Off Principle

**Your greatest strength is often also your greatest weakness.** They're not separate traits - they're the same trait expressing itself in different contexts.

- Manson at 15: Meds fixed grades, cleaned the room, stopped the video games. But also killed his humour, creativity, and weird perspectives
- His teacher's question: *"Are you sure this is worth it?"*
- The deeper question underneath: *"If you're going to be like this for the rest of your life, shouldn't you figure out how to manage it?"*

### The Semmelweis Illustration

Ignaz Semmelweis discovered hand-washing could prevent childbed fever in 1847 Vienna. His stubbornness, disagreeableness, and obsession with detail were:
- **The reason he solved it** - no one else tracked the data or pushed against consensus
- **The reason he couldn't spread the discovery** - he alienated everyone, refused to publish for 13 years, publicly called colleagues murderers
- He died in a mental institution from an infected wound - because doctors hadn't washed their hands

**You cannot separate the genius from the difficult person. They are the same person.**

### Examples of the Same Pattern

| Strength | Weakness |
|----------|----------|
| The selfless mother who builds incredible bonds | Loses herself, needs better boundaries |
| The founder who builds an empire (100hr weeks) | Destroys marriage, barely knows kids |
| The artist whose sensitivity creates moving work | Struggles to function in ordinary life |
| Jordan's neurotic competitiveness = GOAT status | Same intensity becomes a prison post-retirement (Candy Crush obsession) |

### ADHD as Environmental Mismatch

- ADHD traits (distractible, impulsive, novelty-seeking) probably existed forever
- For most of human history, these people were hunters, explorers, the ones who noticed the rustle in the bushes
- Then we built schools that need kids to sit still for hours
- ADHD diagnoses exploded in the 1990s - not because more ADHD brains appeared, but because more environments couldn't tolerate them
- **The DSM is as much cultural as scientific** - homosexuality was a "disorder" until 1973, "drapetomania" was the "disorder" of enslaved people wanting freedom

### The Reframe: Manage, Don't Fix

Manson spent 20 years trying to "fix" himself:
- Self-help books, every system and framework
- Meditation, therapy, psychedelics, trauma work
- Or just trying to outrun it (travel, partying, alcohol)

**None of it worked** because it was all based on the same flawed assumption: that something was fundamentally broken and needed to be changed.

The actual insight (which took him until nearly 40):

> The goal isn't to fix yourself. It's to understand the trade-off inherent in who you already are and put yourself in the environment that maximises that potential.

### Guardrails, Not Cures

- ADHD brains need guardrails - lots of them
- But good guardrails **leverage your talents** rather than suppress them
- They make you a better version of yourself, not a version you hardly recognise
- Manson's ADHD traits that held him back in school were the **exact same traits** that made him successful as a writer:
  - Gets bored easily -> finds new and interesting ways to express ideas
  - Endless need for novelty -> studies strange subjects, makes unusual connections
  - Impulsive, comfortable with risk -> willing to express ideas others are scared to admit

### The Right Question

Not: "How do I fix this?"

But: **"What does a life look like where this trait is an asset rather than a liability?"**

> Good grades are nice. A clean room is nice. Fitting in is nice. But the question is, are they worth it?

## Relating This to Cubing

This connects to why I built this trainer - Rubik's cubing is actually great for ADHD brains:
- Constant novelty (different scrambles every time)
- Short bursts of intense focus
- Clear, measurable progress
- Tactile/physical engagement
- The "hyperfocus" sweet spot - interesting enough to lock in on
- Structured learning (algorithms) with creative application (finding efficient solutions)
